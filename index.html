01 Dissertation direction

Ongoing

From sound expectation to responsive behavior.

My dissertation studies how the form and interaction of virtual, stylized, holographic, and physically impossible objects shape what people expect to hear. It follows that question from expectation, through tolerance for mismatch, to sound behaviors that respond to action and context in XR.

A developing research direction, not a completed claim.

  1. 01

    Expectation

    How do form, material cues, motion, and meaning suggest a sound before it is heard?

  2. 02

    Tolerance

    How far can material and timbre differ before the sound no longer fits the event?

  3. 03

    Behavior

    How should sound react to action, timing, context, and space around an XR object?

Long-term aim

Not another sound-effect generator, but an auditory layer that helps interactive objects acquire sound behavior.

01 Dissertation track

Timbre Change

Prototype · ongoing

A Unity XR prototype for examining material and timbre retargeting through direct object interaction. The current study asks where an impact sound begins to suggest a target material while still retaining the original event.

  • Material perception
  • Audio transformation
  • XR interaction
02 Separate project

MetaMuseum

Pilot completed · follow-up in development

A KAIST–NYU project on curated auditory design for cross-cultural VR museums. A completed pilot tested spatialized auditory traces from unseen visitors; follow-up work is considering source legibility, artifact sound, and place ambience.

  • Spatial audio
  • Cultural XR
  • Sound curation
03 Related study

Action-Aware Reverberation

Experiment build · ongoing

A controlled VR study asking whether action mode and sound intensity change the reverberation setting people choose for a virtual auditorium.

  • Embodied VR
  • Action contingency
  • Reverberation

03 About

Research · Prototyping · Sound

Sound is part of an object’s behavior.

I am an XR audio and HCI researcher at KAIST. I study how sound gives virtual objects material, action, and presence.

My work connects auditory perception, direct interaction, spatial audio, and generative tools to study how virtual objects become coherent and responsive.
